What is a VQE?
    Uses classical and quantum computing in tandem to accomplish a task with the following components:
        Operator: often a Hamiltonian 'H' that represents the aspect of your system which you seek to optimize - VQE seeks 
        to find the "ground state" eigenvector which correspionds to the minimum eigenvalue
            Hamiltonian: generally used in physics to descirbe energy states but generalizes in quantum computing to a 
            business or logistical problem - can be solved on small scal ebut becomes incalculable for large systems
        
        Ansatz: The family of quantum circuits which are instantiated to approximate the eigenvalue you are seeking via the 
        combined quantum state
            In VQE: the quantum circuit used to approximate the multi-qubit wave function whihc best approximates the "ground
            state of the system in question an dproduces the lowest expectation value of 'H'
        
        Estimator: Means of estimating the cost function (expectation value) of the operator 'H' 
            Expecttation value: probability-weighted average of the system's eigenenergies
        
        A classical optimizer to minimize the cost function by varying parameters

Breakdown:
    Initally a theta parameter vector on which the unitary operation of the ansatz is called U_var(theta_0)
    
    While the lowest energy could be estimated simply by measuring repeatedly and smapling the lowest, we lkely also want the
    configuration which yeilds the lowest energy/eigenvalue. As such, we take quantum measurements to estimate the
    ecpecttation value of 'H'

    By making many measurements of the Pauli operators making up the Hamiltonian, we estimate the expectation value in the 
    current state

    It is often advantageous to start the system from a better guess than |0>⊗N, such as by using the Hartree-Fock state

    Since one cannot often measure the entire Hmailtonian directly due to non-commuting Pauli X, Y, and Z operators, we 
    measure groups of operators simultaneosuly and combine the results to obtain the expectation value. 
        IBM Quantum primitive Estimator can be used to perform this task efficiently

    Classical optimizers are utilized to search through the parameter space to minimze the output of a particular function,
    with some utilizing gradient information and others not. Commonly used optimizers include Adam, AMSGrad, and SPSA, which
    are each useful in noisy environments like qubit measurements

    A common workflow is to pass the cost function, the set of parameters, and the arguments suhc as the ansatz, Hamiltonian, 
    and Estimator into a minimzer like scipy minimize() with the minimizer method specified

    Since a variational state can never yield an eignevalue lower than that of the ground state, the aproximation relies on
    closeness to this threshold

    In classical computing, it is computationally trivial to set or update parameters, however it is challenging to find 
    relevant ones that are physically motivated. Additionally, the most expensve single step is the calculation of the 
    expectation value for the given state since this involves an NxN matrix acting on a vector of N elements

Quantum workflow:
    On a quantum computer, one could prepare the quantum state using the variational ansatz and then make measurements.

    Summing the weighted measurements of commuting Pauli strings allows not only construction of H but propogated standard 
    deviation and a final expectation value

    There ar epossible imitations in that this is only efficent for relatively small numbers of groups of commuting Pauli
    operators. Given too many, this approach becomes inefficient. Additionally, one might want to perform more operations 
    in general as a way to reduce the uncertainty in a calculation, which is reintroduced through use of a quantum computer.
